Your domain name is the one part of your brand that customers have to type perfectly. A logo can be a bit off and still be recognised; a web address with one wrong letter takes people to an error page or, worse, a competitor. That is why it is worth checking domains while you choose a business name, not afterwards. This guide covers how to choose the right ending, how to judge whether a domain is good enough, and what to do when the one you want is taken.

.ie, .com or .co.uk?

.ie: the local trust signal

The .ie domain is run by the IE Domain Registry (weare.ie). For businesses that mainly serve customers in Ireland, it is usually the best choice: Irish shoppers tend to trust it, and it signals that you are local, deliver here and price in euro. To register one you need to show a connection to the island of Ireland, which includes Northern Ireland; for most businesses that simply means an address here. Since 2018 you no longer need to justify why you want a particular name, which makes registering one much easier than it used to be.

.com: the global default

If you sell online to customers in several countries, or you hope to, a .com is the safe default. It is the ending people guess when they do not know. The downside is that short, meaningful .com names are scarce, which is one reason brandable invented names (like "Kavora" or "Pipely") are popular: they are far more likely to be free.

.co.uk: for UK-focused businesses

For a business in Northern Ireland or Great Britain selling mainly to UK customers, .co.uk does the same job as .ie does here. There is also plain .uk, but .co.uk remains more familiar to most people.

Should you buy more than one?

Often, yes. A sensible small-business setup is to pick one main domain for your website and email, then register one or two others (for example the .com and .ie) and redirect them to the main one. Domains are cheap compared with the cost of someone else picking up your name and confusing your customers.

What makes a good domain name

  • Short: ideally under 15 characters before the dot. Every extra letter is another chance for a typo.
  • Easy to spell after hearing it once: say it out loud to a friend and ask them to type it. If they hesitate ("is that with a k?"), think again.
  • No hyphens or numbers where possible. Hyphens are forgotten, and "4" vs "four" is a coin toss.
  • Close to the business name: exact matches are best; a small, logical addition is fine (see below).
  • No unfortunate word breaks: read it without spaces. Plenty of well-meaning businesses have discovered too late that their name runs together into something rude or silly.

When the domain you want is taken

This happens constantly, so do not panic. You have several reasonable options:

  1. Add a short, natural word. "get", "hello", "try", "shop", "studio", "hq", or your trade: getpipely.com, hellokavora.ie, solasphotography.ie. Keep it to one word, and make sure the result still reads naturally.
  2. Add your location. For local businesses, corkcakecompany.ie or liffeyplumbing.ie is perfectly respectable and may even help customers understand you.
  3. Use a different ending. If the .com is taken but the .ie is free and you serve Irish customers, that may be all you need. Check what is on the .com first: if it is a competitor in your industry, choose a different name rather than a different ending.
  4. Try to buy it. Some taken domains are parked and for sale. Prices range from reasonable to absurd. Set a budget before you ask, and use an escrow service for anything significant.
  5. Go back to the name. If you cannot get any sensible domain, that is a strong sign the name is too generic. Use the generator's "Short & brandable" style to find something more distinctive.

How to check availability quickly

Every result in our business name generator has a "Check domain" button that opens lookups for the .com, .ie and .co.uk, plus the matching Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and X handles. A few notes on reading the results:

  • Registrar search pages are the most reliable way to see whether a domain can be bought right now. Some show "premium" prices for desirable names. That means it is available, but at a much higher price than normal.
  • A WHOIS or RDAP lookup tells you whether a domain is registered and, sometimes, when it expires. Most personal details are now hidden for privacy.
  • Do not search for a domain repeatedly on dodgy "domain checker" sites. Stick to well-known registrars and the official registries.

Buying and protecting your domain

Once you have found the one, a few habits will save you headaches later:

  • Register it in your own name (or your company's), using an email address you control. Many small businesses have lost their domain because a web designer or relative registered it in theirs.
  • Turn on auto-renew and keep your card details up to date. Expired domains are snapped up quickly.
  • Use two-factor authentication on your registrar account.
  • Set up a professional email address (you@yourbusiness.ie). It looks far more trustworthy than a free webmail address on an invoice.
  • Keep a note of where the domain is registered and where it is hosted. They are often different companies, and you will need both if you change web designer.

Do domains still matter for SEO?

Less than they used to. Putting keywords in the domain (for example best-cheap-plumber-dublin.ie) no longer gives you a meaningful ranking boost and can look spammy. What matters far more is having a fast, useful website, a complete Google Business Profile and genuine reviews. Choose the domain that is easiest for humans to remember and type; search engines will follow.

Quick checklist

  1. Pick your main ending based on where your customers are (.ie, .co.uk or .com).
  2. Keep it short, spellable and hyphen-free.
  3. Read it run together to catch awkward word breaks.
  4. Check the matching social handles at the same time.
  5. Register it in your own name, with auto-renew and two-factor authentication on.
  6. Consider buying the other main endings and redirecting them.
