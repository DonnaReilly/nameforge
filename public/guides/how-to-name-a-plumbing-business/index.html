How to Name a Plumbing Business (With 40+ Name Ideas)

By the NameForge team · Updated 2026-11-17 · 5 min read

Most people choose a plumber in a hurry. Water is coming through the ceiling, the boiler has packed in on the coldest night of the year, or the landlord wants the bathroom finished by Friday. In that moment, your business name has about two seconds to do its job: tell the customer you are a plumber, you are local and you can be trusted. That makes naming a plumbing business different from naming a café or a design studio, and it is why some of the "clever" names that work in other industries fall flat in the trades.

This guide walks through how to pick a name that wins those two seconds, with example names you can adapt and the checks you need to do in Ireland and the UK before you paint it on the van.

What customers want from a plumber's name

When we look at how people search for and choose trades, three things come up again and again:

  • Clarity: the word "plumbing", "heating", "gas" or "drains" somewhere in the name or tagline, so nobody has to guess what you do.
  • Locality: a town, county, river or local nickname reassures people you are nearby and will turn up quickly.
  • Reliability: words that suggest you are dependable, tidy and honest. Think "Trusty", "Sure", "Steady" rather than "Xtreme" or "Ninja".

Notice what is not on the list: being funny. A pun can work brilliantly (and people do remember "The Drain Gang"), but it should never get in the way of the three points above. If you want humour, put it in the van graphics or the tagline and keep the core name solid.

Four naming approaches that work for plumbers

1. Your own name plus the trade

"Seán Murphy Plumbing & Heating" is the classic for a reason: it is personal, it signals accountability and it builds a reputation that follows you. The downsides are that it is hard to sell the business later, common surnames are hard to find domains for, and it can feel small if you plan to hire a team. If you go this way in Ireland and trade under your own true name, you do not need to register a business name with the CRO; add anything extra (like "& Sons" or "Heating Solutions") and you do.

2. Place plus trade

"Dublin Plumbing & Heating", "Leeside Plumbers", "Corrib Heating Services". These names are instantly understood and can help with local searches. The catch: generic place-plus-trade names are almost impossible to trademark, many are already taken, and a name tied to one town can limit you if you grow. Local nicknames (Leeside for Cork, Liffey for Dublin, Shannon for Limerick) are a nice middle ground: local but more distinctive.

3. A distinctive word plus the trade

This is often the sweet spot. Pick a memorable word and add a plain descriptor: "Copper Kettle Plumbing", "Bluebell Heating", "Tobar Plumbing" (tobar is Irish for a well). The distinctive word makes you easier to remember and to protect, while the descriptor does the explaining.

4. Benefit or promise names

"Rapid Response Plumbing", "Dry Home Drains", "No Drip Plumbing". These tell customers what they get. They work well on Google Ads and van signage but tend to be generic, so check carefully that no one nearby already uses something similar.

Words to use (and a few to avoid)

Strong plumbing words include flow, tap, pipe, valve, copper, spanner, drain, heat, warm, dry, clear, steady, trusty and sure. Water and heat imagery (kingfisher, otter, spring, well, ember, hearth) can give a name personality without being cheesy.

Be careful with:

  • "Emergency" or "24/7" in the name itself: great if true, a reputational problem if you stop offering night call-outs.
  • Accreditation words such as "Registered", "Certified" or "Gas Safe". In Ireland, gas work must be done by a Registered Gas Installer (RGII), and in the UK by a Gas Safe registered engineer; implying a registration you do not hold is a serious matter.
  • Toilet humour. One or two plumbers have built fun brands around it, but it limits you with commercial customers, landlords and property managers.
  • Initials only such as "JMK Plumbing". They are hard to remember and impossible to search for.

Checks before you commit

Once you have a shortlist of three to five names, work through these checks. It takes an evening and can save you thousands in rebranding.

  1. Google it with your county: "[name] plumbing Kildare". If a competitor appears, move on.
  2. Search the company registers: CRO (via CORE) in Ireland, Companies House in the UK.
  3. Search trademarks: the IPOI, UK IPO and EUIPO registers, looking at class 37 (installation and repair services) and class 11 (heating and plumbing equipment). See our trademark checking guide.
  4. Check the domain and socials: ideally the .ie or .co.uk plus a matching Facebook page, which matters a lot for trades because neighbours share recommendations in local Facebook groups.
  5. Do the van test: write the name in big letters, step back ten metres and read it. Could a driver behind you remember it at the next red light? Is your phone number still the star of the show?

Registering the name

In Ireland, if you trade under any name other than your own, register it with the Companies Registration Office using form RBN1 (for a sole trader) within a month of starting to use it; the online fee is currently €20. If you are forming a limited company, the company name is registered as part of incorporation, and you can reserve it for 28 days beforehand. In the UK, sole traders do not register a trading name, but there are rules about what names can include, and limited companies register with Companies House.

Remember that registering a business or company name does not stop someone else using a similar name. If you are investing seriously in the brand (vans, uniforms, a website), a trademark in class 37 is worth considering.

Final tip: let the name and the phone number work together

For a plumber, the name gets you remembered but the phone number gets you hired. Pick a name short enough that your number, your area and a one-line promise ("Boilers, bathrooms, burst pipes: same-day call-outs in South Dublin") all fit comfortably on the van and the Google Business Profile. If the name is so long that it crowds those out, keep shortening.
