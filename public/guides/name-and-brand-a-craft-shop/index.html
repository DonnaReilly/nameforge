How to Name and Brand a Craft Shop (Etsy, Markets and Online)

By the NameForge team · Updated 2026-11-17 · 4 min read

Craft businesses live and die by personality. People do not buy a hand-poured candle or a sheet of kawaii stickers because they need one; they buy because they fall a little bit in love with the maker and the style. Your shop name is the first place that personality shows up, on Etsy search results, market stall banners, Instagram profiles and the little thank-you card in each parcel. Here is how to choose a name that fits a craft shop, and how to build a simple brand around it.

Start with your style, not your product

Ask yourself what makes your work recognisable. Is it cute and colourful? Earthy and calm? Bold and a bit cheeky? Vintage? A craft shop name that captures a style lasts much longer than one that names a product, because makers almost always branch out. The sticker shop adds washi tape, the candle maker adds wax melts, the colouring-page designer starts selling planners.

Write down three words for your style (for example kawaii, cheerful, cosy) and three for the feeling you want buyers to have (delighted, calm, treated). Use them as keywords in the craft name generator with the "Playful" style and see what comes up.

Name styles that suit craft shops

  • Cute and playful: "Pickle & Pop", "Little Doodle Co.", "Hello Sprout". Food words, small animals and soft sounds (m, b, p, double letters) feel friendly and work beautifully for kawaii, children's and gift products.
  • Maker's name: "Made by Aoife", "Niamh Draws". Personal and warm, great for commissions and portraits. Harder to sell later, and very common, so add something distinctive.
  • Nature and calm: "Willow & Wax", "Fern Paper Studio", "Bláth Ceramics". Ideal for candles, ceramics, botanicals and wellbeing products.
  • Clever compound: "Inkling", "Stitchcraft", "Kawaiink". A blend can give you a short, ownable name, as long as it is easy to say.
  • Irish words: gleoite (lovely, cute), snáth (thread), grá (love), draíocht (magic). Beautiful and distinctive, especially for gifts and tourism markets. Check that customers can say and spell them, and drop the fada in the web address.

Practical limits: Etsy, Instagram and your stall banner

Craft shops often sell across several platforms, each with its own rules:

  • Etsy shop names are limited to 20 characters with no spaces or special characters, and must be unique on Etsy. "PickleAndPopStudio" fits; "ThePickleAndPopStickerStudio" does not. Use capital letters to make run-together names readable.
  • Instagram usernames can be up to 30 characters and may include full stops and underscores; TikTok allows 24; X allows 15. If your name is long, decide on a short version now and use it everywhere.
  • Market banners and labels are read from a distance and at a glance, so short names with clear letterforms win.

Before you commit, check that the same name (or a very close version) is free on Etsy, the main social platforms and as a domain. Matching handles make you much easier to find after someone meets you at a market.

Building a simple brand around the name

You do not need an agency. A small, consistent kit makes a craft shop look established:

Colours

Pick two main colours and one accent, and use them everywhere: shop banner, packaging, social posts. Pastel pinks and mint greens say kawaii; terracotta and sage say earthy; black, white and one bright colour say modern.

Fonts

Choose one display font for your name (rounded for cute, serif for classic, hand-lettered for personal) and one plain font for everything else. Resist using six different fonts on one label.

A mascot or motif

Kawaii and children's brands in particular benefit from a character: a smiling dumpling, a sleepy cat, a little cloud. It gives you endless content for social media and makes packaging instantly recognisable. Motifs (a sprig of fern, a star, a stitched border) do the same for calmer brands.

Packaging and unboxing

Thank-you cards, a branded sticker to seal the parcel, a handwritten note: this is where craft brands win repeat customers and social shares. Put your shop name and handle on everything that goes in the box.

Protecting a craft brand

Craft brands get copied, and popular names on Etsy can clash. A few basics:

  • Search Etsy, Instagram and the trademark registers before you choose. Classes 16 (paper goods and stationery), 28 (toys) and 35 (online retail) are common for craft shops. See our trademark guide.
  • In Ireland, register your trading name with the CRO (form RBN1, €20 online) if it is not your own name.
  • If the brand takes off, a trademark for the name is worth considering, especially before you sell wholesale or on large marketplaces.
  • Your designs are generally protected by copyright automatically, but keep dated sketches and files as evidence.

From hobby to brand: when to rename

Many craft businesses start under a jokey or personal name and later outgrow it. If you are thinking about a rename, the best time is before you invest in printed stock and wholesale accounts. Keep the old social handles, announce the change clearly, and redirect your old shop links where the platform allows it. Loyal customers tend to love being part of the story.
